# Research Notes: Hydrogen Subsurface Storage

## Source 1: Tackie-Otoo & Haq (2024) - Comprehensive Review on Geo-storage of H2 in Salt Caverns

**Citation:** Tackie-Otoo, B. N., & Haq, M. B. (2024). A comprehensive review on geo-storage of H2 in salt caverns: Prospect and research advances. *Fuel*, 356, 129609. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fuel.2023.129609

**Key Findings:**
- Salt caverns are cost-effective and require minimal cushion gas, making them ideal for short-term storage of hydrogen
- Underground hydrogen storage (UHS) in salt caverns has emerged as favorable option due to:
  - High withdrawal capacity
  - Low creation cost
  - Enhanced safety
- Salt caverns exhibit high storage integrity for UHS despite specific properties of hydrogen
- Different salt deformation mechanisms act competitively to prevent significant permeability evolution
- Salt caverns are subjected to high frequency peak load cycling due to better withdrawal capacity
- H2 undergoes changes in temperature and pressure during cycling
- Cited by 136 papers (as of 2024)

**Technical Details:**
- Storage integrity concerns: abiotic (geochemical) and biotic (microbial activities) reactions, fault and fractures, H2 cycling
- Two immiscible phases present in salt cavern lifetime
- Hydrogen migration into neighboring rock domain induced by temperature and pressure changes

**Research Gaps:**
- Further investigations recommended for monitoring and control

## Source 8: Zhao et al. (2024) - Geothermal Heat Transfer Effects

**Citation:** Zhao, T., Chen, Y., Xu, H., Zhang, Y., & Liu, W. (2024). Effect of geothermal heat transfer on performance of the underground compressed air energy storage. *Applied Thermal Engineering*, 248, 123145. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applthermaleng.2024.123145

**Key Findings:**

- Important consideration for underground storage systems
